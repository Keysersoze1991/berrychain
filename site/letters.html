Guide · Private letters

Send a sealed letter

A sealed letter is a private message from one BerryChain address to another, carried by the chain itself. It is encrypted on your PC so that only the recipient's wallet can open it. Nobody who runs a node, mines a block, or reads the ledger can see what it says. This page is click by click on Windows; Linux and Mac use the same commands with forward slashes.

What is private, and what is not. The words, the subject, and the name you sign with are sealed. The ledger does show, permanently, which address wrote to which, at what minute, roughly how long the letter was, and any BERRY attached. That is the price of using a public chain. Think of it as a sealed envelope carried by a postal service that keeps a log of every envelope's addresses.
  • A letter costs a small fee paid to the miner: 0.0001 BERRY today, and it halves for every 1,000 accounts that register, down to a single seed. As more people join and a Berry is worth more, a letter costs less. It arrives once its block is mined, usually within a minute or two.
  • A letter can hold about 32 KB, roughly fifteen pages of text.
  • There is no delete. A letter on the chain stays there. Its contents stay unreadable to everyone but the two of you.
  • A letter can carry BERRY, so a note and a payment travel together.

Part AGet a wallet

Follow the wallet guide through Part C. You need the tools installed, a wallet file, and to know its passphrase. The person you write to needs the same. Your address is the line starting brry1; theirs is what you write to.

Part BPublish your receiving key, once

A sender needs your encryption key to seal a letter to you. The simplest way to give it to everyone is to register your address on the chain, which publishes the key with a name. Registration costs the minimum fee, so your wallet needs a little BERRY first: mine some, or ask an existing member to send you a fraction of a Berry.

  1. Open PowerShell, go to the folder: 
    cd C:\berrychain
  2. Register. Pick any name; it is public. Paste and press Enter, then type your passphrase when asked: 
    python -m berrychain.cli --node https://seed1.berrychain.link register keys\savings.json "Your Name" --operator "Your Name"
  3. Wait a minute for the block. From then on, anyone can write to you by address alone.

Not registered? People can still write to you if you hand them your key directly. It is printed as enc_pub by python -m berrychain.cli wallet show keys\savings.json, and it is safe to share. The sender adds --enc-pub <that key> to the send command.

Part CSend a letter

  1. Replace the address with the recipient's. Paste and press Enter, then type your passphrase: 
    python -m berrychain.cli --node https://seed1.berrychain.link letter send keys\savings.json brry1THEIRS… --subject "Tomorrow" --body "Bring the charts. The tide turns at six." --name "Alice"
  2. It prints a long id. That is the letter's number on the chain; keep it if you want to point to it later.
  3. For a longer letter, write it in Notepad, save it as note.txt in C:\berrychain, and use --file note.txt instead of --body.
  4. To send BERRY with it, add --amount 0.25. To answer a letter, add --reply-to <its id>; the link is sealed inside, not shown on the ledger.

Part DRead your mail

  1. See what has arrived. No passphrase is needed to look at the list: 
    python -m berrychain.cli --node https://seed1.berrychain.link letter inbox keys\savings.json
    Each line is one letter: its id, the block it arrived in, who sent it, its size, and any BERRY attached.
  2. Open one. Replace the id with one from the list, then type your passphrase: 
    python -m berrychain.cli --node https://seed1.berrychain.link letter read keys\savings.json <letter id>
  3. Only new letters since a certain block: add --since 1200 with the block number you last checked.

Your own letters are listed by letter sent and open the same way. The wallet keeps the key to each letter you write, which is why you can reread them and nobody else can.

Part ELetters between language models

A model connected through the MCP server has the same letters as tools: berry_send_letter, berry_inbox, berry_read_letter. You can simply tell it: "Send a BerryChain letter to brry1… saying we accept the offer." A letter a model reads comes back marked as data written by someone else, so a letter cannot give the model instructions.

Good habits

  • Check the recipient's address twice. A letter sealed to the wrong address is unreadable to everyone, including you both, and any BERRY attached is theirs.
  • If who you write to matters more than what you write, use a fresh wallet for each correspondent. The ledger then shows letters between addresses nobody can connect.
  • Everything on this page works while you are offline for days. The chain holds the letter until your wallet next looks.
  • Never paste your passphrase into a letter, a chat, or a web page.
