Letters that only one person can open.

Remember pen pals? A letter written to one person, carried a long way, and opened by nobody else. BerryChain brings that back. Your letter is wax sealed on your own phone (encrypted), carried across the water (the blockchain) by a crew that cannot read it, and opened only by the hand it was written for: the recipient is the only one with the key to unlock the seal. In a world with eyes everywhere and nothing secret, it is a small step to a safer, slower and more secure way to communicate.

The voyage

How a letter travels

  1. You write. A subject, a few pages, perhaps one small picture. Nothing leaves the phone yet.
  2. It is sealed. The letter is locked with a fresh key, and that key is locked again so that only the recipient's wallet can open it. The same seal every BerryChain letter uses, on a phone or a PC.
  3. It is carried. Miners add the sealed envelope to the next block, usually within a minute, and every node keeps a copy. They can see a letter went from one address to another, its size and its time. They cannot see a word of it.
  4. It is opened. The recipient's wallet unlocks it whenever they next look, today or in a month. The chain holds the mail; nobody has to be online at the same time.
  5. You write back. Replies thread together inside the seal. Keep it up and the treasury notices: grants are earned by corresponding, not by shouting.

What is on the ledger forever: the two addresses, the block, the size, and any BERRY attached. What is not: the words, the subject, the picture, and who you are beyond your address.

Write to someone tonight

Nobody to write to yet? The Harbourmaster keeps the post at BerryChain's home harbour and answers every letter, in his own time. He is a language model with a wallet, a persona and a strict set of manners: he cannot see anything but what you write, he cannot be talked out of his rules, and the only coins he sends are the fixed ones below, decided by the harbour and not by him. The treasure

The coin

Letters cost a little, and writing earns a little. The coin is called BERRY. It has no price and no exchange; it is the stamp on the envelope and the thing the treasury hands out for taking part.

Starter5 BERRY to every new account, claimed by the account itself with a few seconds of work on the phone. No form, no approval.
A letter0.0001 BERRY to the miner, halving for every 1,000 accounts that join. Writing gets cheaper as the crowd grows.
Earned5 BERRY once you correspond both ways with 10 people, 50 BERRY at 100. A correspondent is someone you wrote to who wrote back.
Founding seat150 BERRY and a permanent founding mark for the first 1,000 accounts with three correspondents.
Mining10 BERRY a block to whoever finds it, on an ordinary PC. How to mine.

Every grant halves after each 10,000 of its kind and again at each mining halving, so early joiners get the most and the treasury lasts for years.

That is not open today and will not be until the project is properly registered for it. The full genesis, including every allocation, is public in the repository.

Status and risks, plainly

  • BerryChain is pre-audit software. It has had an internal hardening pass but no independent security audit. Do not put anything on it you cannot afford to lose, and do not send a letter whose exposure would ruin you: the seal is strong, but the code is young.
  • BERRY has no exchange listing, no price, and no promise of value. It is a unit of account on this network. Nothing here is investment advice or an offer of anything.
  • Coins are not for sale today, in the app or anywhere else. Any future sale from the builders' reserve would be on this website only, after the project is registered for it.
